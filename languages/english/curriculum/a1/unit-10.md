# A1 · Unit 10 — Weather, clothes & seasons

**Can-do:** Can describe the weather and what people are wearing, and join simple ideas with basic connectors.

## Grammar focus
Present continuous review (for weather and clothes now) and the connectors *and / but / because*. Talk about weather with *It's…* or the present continuous: *It's cold. It's raining.* Describe clothes now with **be + wearing**: *She is wearing a coat.* Use **and** to add ideas, **but** to show contrast, and **because** to give a reason: *It's cold and windy. It's sunny but cold. I'm wearing a coat because it's cold.*

- It's raining today.
- The sun is shining.
- She is wearing a warm coat.
- I'm wearing a hat because it's cold.
- It's hot and sunny.
- It's cold but nice.
- We aren't wearing shoes.
- What are you wearing today?

## Target vocabulary (10–12 items)
rain — water that falls from the sky
sun — the bright star that gives us light and heat
snow — soft white flakes that fall when it is very cold
wind — air that moves outside
hot — having a high temperature
cold — having a low temperature
shirt — clothing for the top of the body
shoes — you wear these on your feet
coat — a warm piece of clothing for outside
hat — clothing you wear on your head
summer — the warm season of the year
winter — the cold season of the year

## Functions & phrases
- Describing weather: "It's raining." / "It's very cold today."
- Describing clothes: "I'm wearing a coat." / "She has a red hat."
- Connecting ideas: "It's cold and wet." / "It's sunny but windy."
- Giving reasons: "I'm wearing a coat because it's cold."

## Suggested activities
- reading: a short weather report for two different cities and seasons
- conversation: describe today's weather and what you and others are wearing
- quiz focus: weather with it's/present continuous, be + wearing, and and/but/because

## Watch out
- Today is raining → It's raining today / It is raining
- She wears a coat now → She is wearing a coat (right now)
- It's cold, because I wear a hat → I'm wearing a hat because it's cold
