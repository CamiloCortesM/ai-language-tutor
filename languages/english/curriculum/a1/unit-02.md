# A1 · Unit 02 — People & family

**Can-do:** Can talk about my family, say who people are, and describe what I have using simple possessives and numbers.

## Grammar focus
*Have got* (possession) and possessive adjectives. Use **have got** for I/you/we/they and **has got** for he/she/it: *I have got two sisters. She has got a dog.* Negatives: *haven't got / hasn't got.* Questions put *have/has* first: *Have you got a brother?* Possessive adjectives (**my, your, his, her, its, our, their**) come before a noun. Regular plurals add **-s** (brother → brothers), and words ending in *-s, -x, -ch, -sh* add **-es** (box → boxes).

- I have got a big family.
- She has got two children.
- We haven't got a car.
- He hasn't got a sister.
- Have you got any brothers? — Yes, I have.
- Has she got a baby? — No, she hasn't.
- This is my mother and that is her husband.
- Their parents live near us.

## Target vocabulary (10–12 items)
mother — your female parent
father — your male parent
sister — a girl with the same parents as you
brother — a boy with the same parents as you
son — someone's male child
daughter — someone's female child
parents — your mother and father
baby — a very young child
wife — a married woman (partner)
husband — a married man (partner)
grandmother — your parent's mother
grandfather — your parent's father

## Functions & phrases
- Describing family: "I have got two brothers." / "She has got a big family."
- Asking about family: "Have you got any sisters?" / "How many children have you got?"
- Saying who people are: "This is my father." / "Her name is Emma, my daughter."
- Using numbers: "I have got three cousins." / "We are five people at home."

## Suggested activities
- reading: a short family description with names and how many people are in the family
- conversation: show a family photo and describe each person
- quiz focus: has got vs. have got, possessive adjectives, and forming regular plurals

## Watch out
- She have got a son → She has got a son
- Is my father brother → He is my father's brother / my brother
- Two childs → two children
