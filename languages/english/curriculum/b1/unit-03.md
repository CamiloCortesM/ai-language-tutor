# B1 · Unit 03 — Storytelling

**Can-do:** I can tell a story about the past and make clear which events happened before other events.

## Grammar focus
The **past perfect** (*had + past participle*) is the "past before the past". When you tell a story in the past simple and need to step further back to an earlier event, use the past perfect (*By the time I arrived, the train had left*). It makes the order of events clear without dates. Good narratives combine tenses: the **past continuous** sets the scene and background (*It was raining, people were hurrying*), the **past simple** carries the main events (*then the lights went out*), and the **past perfect** fills in what had already happened before them. Signal words like *by the time, before, after, already, just, when* help you place events in order.

- When we **got** to the cinema, the film **had** already **started**.
- She **couldn't** pay because she'**d left** her wallet at home.
- The sun **was shining** and the birds **were singing**.
- After he'**d finished** the chapter, he **turned** off the light.
- I **recognised** her — we'**d met** once before.
- They **had** never **seen** the sea until they **visited** the coast.
- By the time help **arrived**, the storm **had passed**.
- He **told** me a story I'**d** never **heard**.

## Target vocabulary (10–12 items)
novel — a long written story in a book
plot — the series of events that make up a story
character — a person in a story or film
chapter — one of the parts a book is divided into
author — the person who writes a book
fairy tale — a traditional children's story with magic
legend — an old story about heroes, often not fully true
scene — a single part of the action in a film or play
twist — a surprising change in a story
narrator — the voice that tells a story
imagine — to form a picture of something in your mind
turn out — to happen or end in a particular way

## Functions & phrases
- Starting a story: "It all began when…", "You'll never guess what happened."
- Ordering events: "Before that, I'd already…", "By the time we got there…"
- Keeping a listener interested: "And then, suddenly…", "Guess what happened next."
- Ending: "In the end, it turned out fine."

## Suggested activities
- reading: a short anecdote that uses past simple, past continuous and one past perfect flashback.
- conversation: tell a partner about a memorable day, using "by the time…" at least once.
- quiz focus: choosing past perfect vs past simple to show which event came first.

## Watch out
- When I arrived, the film started already → When I arrived, the film had already started
- I had went home before she called → I had gone home before she called
- After I finished, I had gone to bed → After I had finished, I went to bed
