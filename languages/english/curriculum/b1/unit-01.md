# B1 · Unit 01 — Milestones & life stories

**Can-do:** I can tell the story of my life so far, linking finished events to their effect on my life now.

## Grammar focus
The core B1 contrast: **present perfect** vs **past simple**. Use the past simple for a finished action at a finished time — say *when*, and the past simple is your tense (*I graduated in 2019*). Use the present perfect (*have/has + past participle*) when the time is unstated or unfinished, or when a past event still matters now (*I've graduated, so I'm looking for work*). Time markers point the way: *ago, last, in + year, when* pull the past simple; *for, since, so far, recently, up to now, already, yet* pull the present perfect. A common life-story pattern is to open with the present perfect (*I've lived in three countries*) and then switch to the past simple to give the finished details (*I moved to Spain in 2018*).

- I'**ve had** the same best friend since school.
- We **got married** in 2021.
- She'**s changed** a lot since university.
- They **moved** abroad two years **ago**.
- **Have** you **ever** missed an important milestone?
- He **left** home when he **was** eighteen.
- Life '**s taught** me a lot **so far**.
- I **met** my partner at a wedding **last summer**.

## Target vocabulary (10–12 items)
milestone — an important moment or stage in your life
graduate — to finish your studies at a school or university
engaged — having agreed to marry someone
retire — to stop working at the end of your career
generation — all the people born around the same time
upbringing — the way your parents raised you as a child
relative — a member of your family
neighbour — a person who lives near you
memory — something you remember from the past
grow up — to change from a child into an adult
settle down — to start a stable life in one place
look back — to think about the past

## Functions & phrases
- Telling a life story: "I've had quite a journey.", "A lot has happened since then."
- Giving finished details: "I moved there in 2015.", "That was the year I started work."
- Asking about someone's past: "Have you always lived here?", "When did you two meet?"
- Reflecting: "Looking back, it was the right choice."

## Suggested activities
- reading: a short life-story profile that mixes present perfect ("I've done…") with past simple details ("In 2018 I…").
- conversation: interview a partner about three life milestones and when each happened.
- quiz focus: choosing present perfect vs past simple by spotting the time marker.

## Watch out
- I have graduated last year → I graduated last year
- We know each other since 2019 → We have known each other since 2019
- When have you met him? → When did you meet him?
