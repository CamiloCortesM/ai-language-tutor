# B1 · Unit 10 — People & places

**Can-do:** I can give extra information about people, things and places in one sentence using relative clauses.

## Grammar focus
**Defining relative clauses** identify exactly which person, thing or place you mean; without them the sentence is incomplete or unclear. Join two ideas with a relative pronoun: **who** for people, **which** for things, **that** for people or things, **where** for places, and **whose** for possession (*The guide who showed us round was great. The hotel where we stayed was tiny.*). When the relative pronoun is the object of the clause, you can leave it out (*the city (that) we visited*). Do not add an extra pronoun — say *the man who called me*, not *the man who he called me*. Defining clauses take no commas.

- The friend **who** invited me lives abroad.
- That's the app **which** helps you find cheap flights.
- This is the town **where** I grew up.
- She's the traveller **whose** blog I follow.
- The hotel **that** we booked was lovely.
- Do you know anyone **who** speaks Portuguese?
- It's a place **where** the food is amazing.
- The bag **(that)** I lost was never found.

## Target vocabulary (10–12 items)
neighbourhood — an area of a town where people live
destination — the place you are travelling to
landmark — a well-known building or feature people recognise
resident — a person who lives in a particular place
abroad — in or to a foreign country
tourist — a person who visits a place on holiday
local — belonging to the area you are in
scenery — the natural features of an area you can see
accommodation — a place to stay, such as a hotel or flat
crowded — full of a lot of people
sightseeing — visiting interesting places as a tourist
put up — to let someone stay in your home

## Functions & phrases
- Defining people and things: "She's the one who…", "It's the kind of place where…"
- Recommending: "There's a café that does great coffee.", "You should visit the town where…"
- Describing a trip: "The best part was the area where…", "We met a guide who…"
- Asking for specifics: "Which is the museum that everyone talks about?"

## Suggested activities
- reading: a travel review full of defining relative clauses describing people and places.
- conversation: describe your neighbourhood using "It's a place where…" and "There's a shop that…".
- quiz focus: choosing who/which/that/where/whose and spotting when the pronoun can be dropped.

## Watch out
- The man which called me → The man who called me
- The city where I visited it was lovely → The city that I visited was lovely
- She's the woman who she helped us → She's the woman who helped us
