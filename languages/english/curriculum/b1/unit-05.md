# B1 · Unit 05 — Imagine

**Can-do:** I can talk about imaginary or unlikely situations and their results, and say what I would do.

## Grammar focus
The **second conditional** talks about unreal, unlikely or imaginary present and future situations: *If + past simple, … would + verb* (*If I won the lottery, I'd travel the world*). The past-tense form here does not mean past time — it marks the situation as hypothetical. Use **would** (*'d*) for the imaginary result, and **could** or **might** for ability or possibility (*I could quit my job / I might move abroad*). With the verb *be*, careful speakers use **were** for all persons in the *if*-clause (*If I were you…*), the classic way to give advice. Compare with the first conditional: first = real and likely, second = unreal or unlikely.

- If I **had** more money, I'**d** invest it.
- What **would** you do if you **lost** your job?
- If she **were** here, she'**d** know the answer.
- I'**d** buy a house if I **could** afford it.
- If we **spent** less, we'**d** save more.
- He **might** help us if we **asked** him.
- If I **were** you, I'**d** open a savings account.
- They **wouldn't** worry so much if they **had** a budget.

## Target vocabulary (10–12 items)
afford — to have enough money to pay for something
lottery — a game where people win money by chance
wealthy — having a lot of money
budget — a plan for how to spend your money
invest — to put money into something to make more later
borrow — to take money you will pay back later
owe — to have to pay money back to someone
bargain — something bought for less than its usual price
donate — to give money or goods to help others
worth — having a particular value
save up — to keep money to use later
cut back — to spend or use less than before

## Functions & phrases
- Hypothesising: "Imagine if…", "Just suppose you…"
- Giving advice: "If I were you, I'd…", "I wouldn't do that."
- Talking about wishes and dreams: "I'd love to…", "It would be amazing to…"
- Considering options: "I might…", "I could always…"

## Suggested activities
- reading: a "what would you do with a million?" text full of second-conditional sentences.
- conversation: swap answers to "If you could change one thing, what would it be?"
- quiz focus: forming the second conditional and choosing would / could / might.

## Watch out
- If I would have money, I would travel → If I had money, I would travel
- If I will be rich, I will help → If I were rich, I would help
- If I was you, I'd wait → If I were you, I'd wait
