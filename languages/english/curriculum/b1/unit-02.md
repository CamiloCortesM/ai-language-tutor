# B1 · Unit 02 — Work in progress

**Can-do:** I can talk about activities that started in the past and are still going on, and say how long they have lasted.

## Grammar focus
The **present perfect continuous** (*have/has + been + -ing*) describes an action that began in the past and is still happening, or has just stopped with a visible result now. It puts the focus on the activity and its duration rather than a finished result. Use **for** with a length of time (*for two hours, for years*) and **since** with a starting point (*since Monday, since I was a child*). Compare it with the present perfect simple: the continuous stresses "how long / still going" (*I've been studying all day*), while the simple stresses the completed amount (*I've studied three chapters*). Stative verbs like *know, have, be* stay simple.

- I'**ve been working** here **for** six months.
- She'**s been learning** to code **since** January.
- We'**ve been waiting** for the manager all morning.
- **Have** you **been looking** for a new job?
- He'**s tired** because he'**s been commuting** since dawn.
- They'**ve been trying** to reach you all week.
- I'**ve been meaning** to update my CV **for** ages.
- It'**s been raining**, so the site is closed.

## Target vocabulary (10–12 items)
colleague — a person you work with
deadline — the time by which work must be finished
commute — the daily journey to and from work
overtime — extra hours worked beyond the normal day
promotion — a move to a higher, better-paid job
shift — a set period of working time
apply — to formally ask for a job
CV — a document listing your work history and skills
income — the money you earn regularly
skilled — good at a job because of training or practice
freelance — working for yourself, not one company
keep up — to move at the same speed as others

## Functions & phrases
- Saying how long: "I've been doing this for years.", "She's been here since April."
- Talking about progress: "It's coming along.", "We're getting there slowly."
- Asking about someone's work: "How long have you been working there?", "What have you been up to?"
- Explaining a result: "I'm exhausted — I've been working overtime."

## Suggested activities
- reading: an email update where a colleague explains what they've been working on lately.
- conversation: ask a partner how long they've been doing their studies, job or hobby, using for/since.
- quiz focus: for vs since, and present perfect continuous vs simple.

## Watch out
- I work here since March → I have been working here since March
- She has been knowing him for years → She has known him for years
- I've been working there for three years ago → I've been working there for three years
