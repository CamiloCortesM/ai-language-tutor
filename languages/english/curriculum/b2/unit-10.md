# B2 · Unit 10 — Emphasis & flow

**Can-do:** I can add emphasis and connect ideas smoothly using inversion, cleft sentences and discourse markers.

## Grammar focus
**Inversion** puts the auxiliary before the subject after a fronted negative adverbial, for emphasis: *Never, Rarely, Seldom, Not only… (but also), No sooner… than, Hardly… when, Only then* ("**Never have I seen** such a mess"; "**Not only did he apologise**, but he also paid"). **Cleft sentences** split one idea to spotlight a part: *It was… that…* ("**It was** the price **that** shocked me") and *What… is…* ("**What I need is** a break"). **Discourse markers** organise argument and attitude: *however, on the other hand, admittedly, in fact, moreover, as a result, to be honest*.

- **Never have I heard** such a weak excuse.
- **Not only was** the film long, **but it was** also dull.
- **No sooner had** we sat down **than** the phone rang.
- **Only after** the meeting **did I understand** the plan.
- **It was** her attitude **that** impressed the judges.
- **What surprised me most was** the ending.
- **Admittedly**, the idea has flaws; **however**, it's worth trying.

## Target vocabulary (10–12 items)
persuasive — good at making people agree or believe
misleading — giving a wrong idea or impression
target audience — the group a message is aimed at
slogan — a short, memorable phrase used in advertising
endorse — to publicly support or recommend something
manipulate — to influence people unfairly or dishonestly
appeal — the quality of being attractive or interesting
bias — an unfair preference for or against something
exaggerate — to make something seem bigger or better than it really is
credibility — the quality of being trusted or believed
subtle — not obvious; working in a quiet, clever way
compelling — so strong or interesting that it convinces you

## Functions & phrases
- Emphasising: "What really matters is…", "It's not the cost, it's the principle."
- Structuring argument: "On the one hand… on the other hand…"
- Conceding then countering: "Admittedly it's risky, but even so, I'd try it."

## Suggested activities
- reading: an opinion column using cleft sentences and discourse markers.
- conversation: debate whether an advert is honest, emphasising key points with clefts.
- quiz focus: forming inversion after negative adverbials; building *It / What* clefts.

## Watch out
- Never I have seen that → Never have I seen that
- No sooner we arrived than it rained → No sooner had we arrived than it rained
- It was the price what shocked me → It was the price that shocked me
- What I need are a break → What I need is a break
