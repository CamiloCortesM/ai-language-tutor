# B2 · Unit 01 — Regrets & what-ifs

**Can-do:** I can talk about past regrets and imagine how the past could have been different, using the third conditional and *wish / if only*.

## Grammar focus
The third conditional imagines an unreal past and its unreal result: *if + past perfect, would have + past participle*. Both halves are finished and cannot change, so we use it to reflect on what did not happen. Use *wish / if only + past perfect* to regret a past action ("I wish I had studied"), but *wish / if only + past simple* to regret a present situation ("I wish I knew the answer"). *If only* is stronger and more emotional than *wish*. The contraction *'d* can mean *had* or *would*, but the *would have* half never sits inside the *if* clause.

- If I **had known** the risk, I **wouldn't have invested** so much.
- She **would have passed** if she **had revised** the last chapter.
- I **wish** I **had accepted** that job offer.
- **If only** we **had left** earlier, we wouldn't have missed the train.
- He wishes he **hadn't said** those words.
- I wish I **were** more patient with people.
- **If only** I **understood** how she feels.
- **Had** I realised sooner, things might have been different.

## Target vocabulary (10–12 items)
regret — a sad feeling about something you did or failed to do
in hindsight — seeing clearly only after something has happened
turn down — to refuse an offer or request
overlook — to fail to notice or consider something
hesitate — to pause before acting because you are unsure
impulsive — acting suddenly without thinking first
resent — to feel angry about something you think is unfair
dwell on — to keep thinking about something unpleasant
reckless — doing dangerous things without caring about the risk
come to terms with — to accept a difficult situation
missed opportunity — a good chance that you did not take
apologise — to say sorry for something you did

## Functions & phrases
- Expressing regret: "I really wish I hadn't…", "If only I had…"
- Imagining alternatives: "Things would have been different if…"
- Softening blame: "In hindsight, I should have seen it coming."
- Reassuring: "There's no point dwelling on it now."

## Suggested activities
- reading: a first-person story about a decision the writer regrets.
- conversation: "biggest what-if" — describe one past choice and how life would have gone differently.
- quiz focus: third conditional form vs *wish + past perfect* (past regret) vs *wish + past simple* (present regret).

## Watch out
- If I would have known → If I had known
- I wish I would have called → I wish I had called
- If only I have more time → If only I had more time
- She would have came → She would have come
