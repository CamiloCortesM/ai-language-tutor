# B2 · Unit 03 — Deep narrative

**Can-do:** I can tell a detailed story, showing how long something had been going on before another past event, and combine actions smoothly.

## Grammar focus
Use the **past perfect continuous** (*had been + -ing*) for an activity in progress up to a point in the past, often to explain a result ("She was exhausted because she **had been travelling** all night"). It contrasts with the past perfect simple, which focuses on completion rather than duration. **Participle clauses** join two ideas without a conjunction when both halves share the same subject: a present participle (*-ing*) replaces an active clause ("**Hearing** the news, she smiled") and a past participle replaces a passive one ("**Shocked** by the result, he left"). *Having + past participle* shows one action finished before the next.

- They **had been arguing** for hours before they reached a decision.
- His eyes were red; he **had been crying**.
- We **had been waiting** for twenty minutes when the bus finally came.
- **Opening** the letter, she realised it was too late.
- **Not knowing** the way, we stopped to ask for directions.
- **Exhausted** after the climb, they set up camp.
- **Having finished** the report, he went straight home.

## Target vocabulary (10–12 items)
gradually — slowly, over a period of time
eventually — in the end, after a long time
suspect — to believe something is true without proof
witness — a person who sees an event happen
tension — a nervous or strained feeling in a situation
reveal — to make known something that was hidden
overwhelmed — feeling unable to cope with too much emotion or work
flashback — a scene showing something that happened earlier
narrator — the person who tells a story
unfold — to develop or happen gradually
motive — a reason for doing something
coincidence — when things happen together by chance

## Functions & phrases
- Sequencing: "Up to that point…", "By the time…", "It was only later that…"
- Explaining background: "The reason was that they had been arguing for days."
- Building suspense: "Little did they know what was coming."

## Suggested activities
- reading: a short suspense extract using past perfect continuous and participle openings.
- conversation: retell a memorable event, saying what had been happening beforehand.
- quiz focus: past perfect simple vs continuous; forming participle clauses from full clauses.

## Watch out
- I was tired because I worked all day → I was tired because I had been working all day
- Having ate dinner, we left → Having eaten dinner, we left
- Walking home, the rain started → Walking home, I got caught in the rain
- She had been knowing him for years → She had known him for years
