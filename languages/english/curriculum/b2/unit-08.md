# B2 · Unit 08 — Adding layers

**Can-do:** I can add extra, non-essential information to a sentence smoothly using relative clauses.

## Grammar focus
**Non-defining relative clauses** give extra information that could be removed without changing who or what you mean; they sit between commas and never use *that* ("My boss, **who is from Canada**, speaks four languages"). Use *which* to comment on a whole clause ("He was late, **which annoyed everyone**"). **Reduced relative clauses** drop the relative pronoun and *be*: an active clause becomes an *-ing* participle ("the man **who is waiting**" → "the man **waiting**"), and a passive one becomes a past participle ("the car **that was stolen**" → "the car **stolen**"). Reducing clauses this way tightens formal writing.

- Venice, **which attracts millions of tourists**, is slowly sinking.
- My neighbour, **whose son is a pilot**, travels a lot.
- The report, **which was published yesterday**, caused an outcry.
- She missed the deadline, **which surprised us all**.
- The passengers **waiting on the platform** looked annoyed.
- Any items **left unattended** will be removed.
- The scientist, **whom I met at the conference**, later won a prize.

## Target vocabulary (10–12 items)
renowned — famous and admired for something
controversial — causing strong public disagreement
pioneering — being the first to develop something new
acclaimed — praised publicly by many people
prominent — important and well known
underestimate — to think something is smaller or weaker than it is
influential — having the power to affect people or events
heritage — the traditions and objects passed down from the past
exhibit — an object shown to the public in a gallery or museum
masterpiece — an outstanding work of art or skill
contemporary — belonging to the present time; modern
restore — to return something to its original condition

## Functions & phrases
- Adding a comment: "…, which is exactly the problem."
- Giving background: "The author, who rarely gives interviews, said…"
- Defining vs describing: "the one that matters" vs "…, which happens to matter"

## Suggested activities
- reading: an art-review paragraph rich in non-defining clauses.
- conversation: describe a famous person or place, adding extra facts in relative clauses.
- quiz focus: commas and *that* vs *which / who*; reducing full relative clauses.

## Watch out
- My father who is a doctor lives in Rome → My father, who is a doctor, lives in Rome
- Rome, that is in Italy, is beautiful → Rome, which is in Italy, is beautiful
- The people waited on the platform were angry → The people waiting on the platform were angry
- He passed the exam, what pleased his parents → He passed the exam, which pleased his parents
