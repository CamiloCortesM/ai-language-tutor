# C1 · Unit 06 — Formal Moves

**Can-do:** I can use the subjunctive and diplomatic phrasing to make demands, recommendations and objections sound formal, measured and tactful.

## Grammar focus
The mandative subjunctive uses the bare infinitive after verbs and adjectives of demand, recommendation and importance — *suggest, insist, recommend, propose, demand, essential that, vital that*: "We insist that he **be** present." It is invariant across all persons, taking no *-s* and no past form. *Were* is the past subjunctive of unreal conditions (*If I were…*, *Were the proposal to fail…*). Formal register also favours *would*, *should*, *if I may* and softened requests. Diplomacy blends the subjunctive with hedging, letting you demand, recommend or disagree without confrontation and leaving your counterpart room to save face.

- The board recommends that the CEO step down immediately.
- It is imperative that every delegate be briefed beforehand.
- She insisted that the clause be removed from the contract.
- We propose that funding be redirected to primary care.
- Were the proposal to fail, we would need a contingency plan.
- I would suggest, if I may, that we revisit the timeline.
- It was essential that no one be informed prematurely.
- Might I respectfully ask that the decision be deferred?

## Target vocabulary (10–12 items)
imperative — of vital importance; absolutely essential
mandate — official authority to act on others' behalf
contingency — a future event that may or may not occur
deferral — the act of postponing something
protocol — the official rules of procedure or etiquette
consensus — general agreement within a group
prudent — acting with care and foresight
tactful — showing sensitivity in dealing with others
reconcile — to make two opposing things compatible
impartial — treating all sides fairly and equally
envoy — a diplomatic representative
overrule — to reject a decision by higher authority

## Functions & phrases
- Formal recommendation: "We would strongly recommend that…"
- Diplomatic disagreement: "With respect, I'm not entirely convinced that…"
- Softened demand: "Might I suggest that…", "It would be advisable that…"
- Face-saving: "There may be some merit in reconsidering…"

## Suggested activities
- reading: a formal proposal or motion — underline every subjunctive and name the trigger word.
- conversation: chair a mock committee, making recommendations with the subjunctive.
- quiz focus: completing subjunctive frames after *suggest / insist / essential* and choosing the diplomatic option.

## Watch out
- I suggest that he goes home. → I suggest that he go home.
- It is essential that she is informed. → It is essential that she be informed.
- If I was you, I'd resign. (formal) → If I were you, I would resign.
