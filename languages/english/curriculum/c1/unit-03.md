# C1 · Unit 03 — What Matters Is…

**Can-do:** I can restructure sentences with cleft constructions and fronting to spotlight the exact element I want the listener to focus on.

## Grammar focus
Cleft sentences split a single idea into two clauses so as to foreground one part. *It*-clefts (*It was X that…*) highlight a specific item and are ideal for contrast and correction. *Wh*-clefts, also called pseudo-clefts (*What I need is…*), foreground an action or idea and typically save the new information for the end. Related patterns — *The reason… / The thing… / The place… is* — work the same way. Fronting simply moves a phrase to the start for emphasis or cohesion, usually without inversion. Together these devices let you manage focus and contrast across extended discourse, steering the listener to your point.

- It was globalisation, not automation, that hollowed out these towns.
- What strikes me most is the sheer scale of the inequality.
- All I'm asking for is a fair hearing.
- It wasn't until the 1970s that the theory gained traction.
- The reason the scheme failed was a lack of political will.
- What the committee did next surprised everyone.
- Deeply flawed though the plan was, it went ahead.
- Such was the outcry that the law was repealed.

## Target vocabulary (10–12 items)
inequality — an unfair difference in status, rights or wealth
disparity — a great and often unjust difference
scrutiny — close and critical observation
accountability — the state of being answerable for one's actions
transparency — openness and the absence of concealment
vested interest — a personal stake in a particular outcome
paramount — more important than anything else
underpin — to support or form the basis of something
erode — to gradually wear away or weaken
entrenched — firmly established and difficult to change
disenfranchise — to deprive someone of a right or privilege
grievance — a real or perceived cause for complaint

## Functions & phrases
- Highlighting the key point: "What really matters here is…"
- Correcting or contrasting: "It's not X we object to, but Y."
- Emphatic reassurance: "The one thing I can promise is…"
- Structuring an argument: "What I'd like to do first is…"

## Suggested activities
- reading: an ethics essay — spot the cleft structures and note what each one is emphasising.
- conversation: field challenging questions using "What… is…" to steer the focus of your answers.
- quiz focus: converting plain sentences into *it*-clefts and *wh*-clefts that target a given word.

## Watch out
- What I need are your support. → What I need is your support.
- It is because of the rain the match was cancelled. → It was because of the rain that the match was cancelled.
- The thing what worries me… → What worries me… / The thing that worries me…
