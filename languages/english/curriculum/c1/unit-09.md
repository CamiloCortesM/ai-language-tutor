# C1 · Unit 09 — Time Under the Microscope

**Can-do:** I can exploit fine distinctions of tense and aspect to shade meaning in narrative, reportage and hypothetical accounts.

## Grammar focus
At C1, aspect becomes a stylistic instrument rather than a rule to obey. The continuous frames an action as ongoing, temporary or emotionally coloured (*I'm always losing my keys*); the perfect ties two time points together and signals relevance or completion; the perfect continuous stresses duration and the traces it leaves. Layered onto these are backshift in reported speech, the historic present for vivid narration, future-in-the-past (*was going to*), and past forms used for the "distance" of unreal situations. The narrative past perfect flags anteriority — an event before the main past event; misplace it and the timeline collapses into a flat sequence.

- By the time the report surfaced, the damage had already been done.
- She'd been campaigning for years before anyone took notice.
- So there I am, standing in the rain, when the phone rings.
- I was going to resign, but circumstances changed.
- Prices have been climbing steadily since the spring.
- He kept insisting he'd never seen the document.
- They'll have been married fifty years this June.
- Had I known, I would have acted differently.

## Target vocabulary (10–12 items)
retrospect — a survey or review of past events
anticipate — to expect or foresee something
unfold — to develop or happen gradually
ongoing — still in progress
imminent — about to happen very soon
protracted — lasting longer than expected or usual
belated — coming later than it should have
concurrent — happening at the same time
hindsight — understanding of an event after it has happened
precede — to come before something in time
lapse — a passage of time; a temporary failure
culminate — to reach a climax or highest point

## Functions & phrases
- Vivid narration: "There I was, …", "Next thing I knew, …"
- Reporting shifting claims: "He maintained that he had…"
- Hypothetical past: "Had it not been for…, we would have…"
- Framing duration: "For months now, we've been…"

## Suggested activities
- reading: a news feature — explain why each perfect or continuous form was chosen.
- conversation: recount an anecdote, switching into the historic present for the climax.
- quiz focus: selecting the aspect that fits the nuance (past simple vs past perfect vs perfect continuous).

## Watch out
- When I arrived, they already left. → When I arrived, they had already left.
- I have seen that film last night. → I saw that film last night.
- She is knowing the answer. → She knows the answer.
