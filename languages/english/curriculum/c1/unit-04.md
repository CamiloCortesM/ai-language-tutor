# C1 · Unit 04 — The Unsaid

**Can-do:** I can leave out or replace repeated words with *so*, *neither*, *one*, *do so* and other pro-forms to keep my speech and writing economical and cohesive.

## Grammar focus
Ellipsis omits words the listener can recover from context; substitution replaces them with a pro-form. Use *so / nor / neither* for agreement and short responses (*so do I*, *neither can they*), *one / ones* for countable nouns, and *do so / do it / do that* for whole verb phrases. After verbs like *think, hope, suppose, be afraid*, use *so* or *not* to stand in for a clause (*I hope not*). Auxiliary ellipsis (*She could have, but didn't*) trims repetition while keeping the grammar intact. These devices signal fluency and tight cohesion, but overuse can leave the meaning ambiguous.

- She promised to review the report, and she duly did so.
- "The economy is fragile." — "It is indeed, and so is confidence."
- I've read the abstract but not the full paper.
- Some delegates supported the motion; others didn't.
- "Will the talks collapse?" — "I hope not."
- He wanted the corner office and eventually got one.
- They could have intervened earlier, but chose not to.
- Neither the report nor its authors were credible.

## Target vocabulary (10–12 items)
concede — to admit or grant something reluctantly
stipulate — to specify as a condition of an agreement
leverage — power to influence a situation or outcome
mutual — shared by two or more parties
provisional — arranged temporarily; not yet final
counterpart — a person holding an equivalent role elsewhere
ratify — to give formal approval to an agreement
amenable — open and responsive to suggestion
reciprocal — given or done in return
deadlock — a situation in which no progress is possible
concession — a thing granted, especially in negotiation
overture — an opening proposal or friendly approach

## Functions & phrases
- Agreeing briefly: "So do I.", "Neither would we."
- Avoiding repetition: "I meant to call, but didn't."
- Hedged short answers: "I suppose so.", "I'm afraid not."
- Referring back: "That's a fair point; the other one is weaker."

## Suggested activities
- reading: a negotiation transcript — recover the full meaning behind each ellipted line.
- conversation: hold a mini-negotiation, using *so / neither / one / do so* to stay concise.
- quiz focus: filling gaps with the right pro-form (*so / neither / one / ones / do so*).

## Watch out
- "I'm tired." "So am tired too." → "So am I."
- I have two red pens and a blue. → I have two red pens and a blue one.
- She said she would come but she not did. → …but she didn't.
