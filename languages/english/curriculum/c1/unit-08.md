# C1 · Unit 08 — Weaving Text

**Can-do:** I can manage extended discourse with a wide range of cohesive devices, signposting the relationships between ideas and holding a long turn together.

## Grammar focus
Cohesion binds a text into a whole through three channels: reference (*this, such, the former / the latter*), conjunction (*nevertheless, consequently, hence, whereas*) and lexical chains (repetition, synonyms, superordinate terms). Discourse markers signpost your route through an argument — *to begin with, on the one hand, that said, in short*. Sentence adverbials such as *admittedly, crucially, incidentally* colour the relationship between one idea and the next. Managing a long turn means previewing your structure, holding the floor and closing cleanly. This precision — knowing which linker names which logical relation — is what separates a C1 speaker from a fluent B2 one.

- The former option is cheaper; the latter, more sustainable.
- Admittedly, the plan has flaws; nevertheless, it remains our best option.
- Crucially, no one anticipated the knock-on effects.
- The talks stalled; consequently, the deadline was pushed back.
- On the one hand, growth resumed; on the other, wages stagnated.
- Such a stance, incidentally, is not without precedent.
- To sum up, then, the reforms achieved little of substance.
- This is precisely why the distinction matters.

## Target vocabulary (10–12 items)
notwithstanding — in spite of; despite
conversely — in an opposite or contrasting way
albeit — although; even though
thereby — by that means; as a result of that
hitherto — until this point in time
insofar as — to the extent that
knock-on effect — a secondary, indirect consequence
crux — the decisive or most important point of an issue
tangent — a divergent and unrelated line of thought
premise — a statement taken as the basis for reasoning
juncture — a critical point in time
offset — to counterbalance or compensate for

## Functions & phrases
- Signposting structure: "Let me turn first to…", "That brings me to…"
- Holding the floor: "If I could just finish the point…"
- Conceding then countering: "Granted, …; even so, …"
- Summarising: "The upshot of all this is…"

## Suggested activities
- reading: a long-form essay — map its cohesive chain from one paragraph to the next.
- conversation: give a two-minute uninterrupted turn, signposting each stage aloud.
- quiz focus: choosing the correct linker (*whereas / hence / albeit*) for a given logical relationship.

## Watch out
- In the other hand, … → On the other hand, …
- Despite of the delay, … → Despite the delay, … / In spite of the delay, …
- I'll resume later, meanwhile continue. → …; in the meantime, please continue.
