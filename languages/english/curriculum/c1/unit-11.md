# C1 · Unit 11 — The Art of the Argument

**Can-do:** I can build a persuasive case, concede fairly, rebut counterarguments and use rhetorical moves to prevail in a reasoned debate.

## Grammar focus
Persuasion at C1 marries structure to rhetoric. Signal your claim, support it with evidence, pre-empt objections with the concede-and-counter move (*while it is true that…, this overlooks…*), then rebut with reasoning. Rhetorical devices lend force: the rhetorical question, the tricolon (*of the people, by the people, for the people*), anaphora, and the concessive *granted… but*. Modality grades your commitment to each claim, and conditionals let you test consequences. A fair concession, far from weakening your case, strengthens it — it shows you have weighed the opposing view and still hold your ground.

- While critics have a point about cost, they ignore the long-term savings.
- Is it really beyond us to fund both? I think not.
- Granted, the transition will be painful; the alternative, however, is worse.
- The evidence is clear, consistent and, frankly, overwhelming.
- If we accept that premise, then the conclusion follows inevitably.
- Far from solving the problem, the ban made it worse.
- Nobody disputes the goal; what is at issue is the method.
- We can quibble over figures, but the trend is undeniable.

## Target vocabulary (10–12 items)
rebuttal — a counterargument that refutes a claim
refute — to prove a statement or accusation wrong
compelling — convincing and forceful
fallacy — a flaw in reasoning; a mistaken belief
rhetoric — the art of using language persuasively
undermine — to weaken something gradually or insidiously
substantiate — to support a claim with evidence
corroborate — to confirm with additional evidence
counterargument — an argument offered in opposition to another
sway — to influence or win over
tenable — able to be defended or maintained
dismissive — treating something as unworthy of consideration

## Functions & phrases
- Building a claim: "The central point is this: …"
- Conceding then countering: "Admittedly…, and yet…"
- Rebutting: "That argument doesn't hold up, because…"
- Pressing home: "Which brings us back to my original point."

## Suggested activities
- reading: a persuasive speech — label the claim, evidence, concession and rebuttal.
- conversation: hold a two-sided debate in which you must concede one point before rebutting.
- quiz focus: matching debate moves to their phrases and identifying the logical fallacy in short arguments.

## Watch out
- I refute to accept that. → I refuse to accept that.
- In spite I disagree, … → Much as I disagree, … / Although I disagree, …
- That proofs my point. → That proves my point.
