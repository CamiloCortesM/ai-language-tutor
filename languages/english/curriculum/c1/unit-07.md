# C1 · Unit 07 — Compression

**Can-do:** I can compress two clauses into one using participle and reduced relative clauses, writing more economically and elegantly.

## Grammar focus
Participle clauses replace a full clause with an *-ing* form (active) or an *-ed* form (passive), sharing the subject of the main clause: "**Having finished** the audit, she left." Perfect participles (*having done*) mark a prior action; *being* + past participle gives a passive participle (*Being underfunded, the scheme closed*). Reduced relative clauses drop the relative pronoun and any auxiliary — "the report [that was] published yesterday". These structures tighten prose and vary its rhythm, but the participle's implied subject must match the subject of the main clause; if it doesn't, you produce a dangling modifier that misassigns the action.

- Faced with mounting debt, the firm filed for bankruptcy.
- Having reviewed the evidence, the panel reached a verdict.
- The measures introduced last year have proved ineffective.
- Written in haste, the memo contained several errors.
- Not knowing the terrain, they hired a local guide.
- The candidates shortlisted for the role were all internal.
- Being underfunded, the programme was quietly shelved.
- Once translated, the document lost some of its nuance.

## Target vocabulary (10–12 items)
elegant — pleasingly graceful and refined in style
concise — brief but comprehensive in expression
verbose — using far more words than necessary
succinct — expressed clearly in very few words
coherent — logically connected and internally consistent
draft — a preliminary version of a piece of writing
revise — to reconsider and amend a text
allusion — an indirect or passing reference
prose — ordinary written language, as opposed to verse
excerpt — a short extract from a longer text
render — to translate or represent in another form
convoluted — extremely complex and hard to follow

## Functions & phrases
- Compressing background: "Having said that, …", "Given the circumstances, …"
- Adding cause: "Exhausted by the debate, they adjourned."
- Describing tersely: "the issues raised", "the points discussed"
- Sequencing events: "Once completed, the survey will…"

## Suggested activities
- reading: a literary passage — rewrite three participle clauses as full clauses and compare the rhythm.
- conversation: retell a story using participle openings ("Having arrived…", "Left alone…").
- quiz focus: combining two sentences into one participle or reduced relative clause, and spotting dangling modifiers.

## Watch out
- Walking to the office, the rain started. → Walking to the office, I got caught in the rain.
- Having wrote the essay, he slept. → Having written the essay, he slept.
- The man arrested yesterday he is free. → The man arrested yesterday is free.
