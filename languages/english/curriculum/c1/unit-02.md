# C1 · Unit 02 — Emphasis Engineered

**Can-do:** I can front negative and restrictive adverbials and invert subject and auxiliary to add dramatic emphasis in formal writing and speech.

## Grammar focus
When a negative or restrictive adverbial moves to the front of a clause, the subject and auxiliary invert as in a question. The usual triggers are *hardly / scarcely … when*, *no sooner … than*, *not only … but also*, *never / rarely / seldom*, *little*, *under no circumstances*, *only then / only after / only by*, and *not until*. This is a marked, emphatic structure reserved for formal, literary or rhetorical register — it lends weight and drama that neutral word order cannot. The auxiliary carries the inversion; where there is no auxiliary, supply *do / does / did*. Save it for effect: overuse tips into pomposity.

- Hardly had the minister spoken when the room erupted.
- No sooner had the ceasefire been announced than fighting resumed.
- Not only did profits fall, but the board also lost the shareholders' confidence.
- Seldom have we witnessed such a decisive electoral shift.
- Under no circumstances should the evidence be disclosed.
- Little did they realise how far-reaching the consequences would be.
- Only after the audit was the fraud finally uncovered.
- Not until the treaty was signed did tensions subside.

## Target vocabulary (10–12 items)
decisive — settling an issue conclusively
far-reaching — having wide and important effects
unprecedented — never done or known before
sweeping — extensive; affecting many things at once
mounting — gradually increasing
watershed — a turning point that changes the course of things
backlash — a strong adverse reaction to something
ramifications — the complex consequences of an action
galvanize — to shock or spur into sudden action
eclipse — to surpass or overshadow completely
staunch — loyal and firmly committed
upheaval — a violent or sudden change

## Functions & phrases
- Rhetorical emphasis: "Never before has…", "Rarely do we see…"
- Heightening narrative drama: "No sooner had… than…"
- Strong prohibition: "On no account must…", "Under no circumstances…"
- Building a two-part point: "Not only… but also…"

## Suggested activities
- reading: a political op-ed — find the inversions and rewrite them in neutral order to feel the loss of force.
- conversation: recount a dramatic news event using at least three inverted openings.
- quiz focus: transforming neutral sentences into emphatic inverted ones with correct auxiliary placement.

## Watch out
- Never I have seen such chaos. → Never have I seen such chaos.
- No sooner he arrived than… → No sooner had he arrived than…
- Not only she sings but also dances. → Not only does she sing, but she also dances.
