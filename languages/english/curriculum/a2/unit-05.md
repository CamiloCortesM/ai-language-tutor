# A2 · Unit 05 — Health & body

**Can-do:** I can describe how I feel, say what is wrong, and give and understand simple health advice.

## Grammar focus
*should* (advice) and *have to / must* (obligation). Use *should / shouldn't + base verb* for advice and opinions. Use *must* for strong obligation or a rule the speaker feels is important, and *have to* for obligation that comes from outside (a rule, a situation); *has to* for he/she/it. The negatives differ in meaning: *mustn't* = it is not allowed; *don't have to* = it isn't necessary.

- You **should** drink more water.
- You **shouldn't** go to work with a fever.
- I **must** see a doctor about this pain.
- She **has to** take the medicine twice a day.
- Patients **have to** wait in reception.
- You **mustn't** touch that — it's hot.
- You **don't have to** stay in bed all day.
- **Should** I take something for my headache?

## Target vocabulary (10–12 items)
headache — a pain in your head
fever — a body temperature that is too high
cough — to push air out of your throat with a noise
ache — a dull pain that continues
pain — an unpleasant feeling in the body
symptom — a sign that shows you are ill
medicine — something you take to get better
prescription — a doctor's written note for medicine
appointment — a fixed time to see a doctor
injury — harm to a part of your body
ill — not healthy, sick
recover — to become healthy again

## Functions & phrases
- Saying what's wrong: "I've got a headache.", "My throat hurts.", "I don't feel well."
- Giving advice: "You should rest.", "You shouldn't eat so late."
- Talking about rules/needs: "You have to book an appointment.", "You mustn't smoke here."
- At the doctor's: "What are your symptoms?", "Take this twice a day."

## Suggested activities
- reading: a short dialogue between a patient and a doctor describing symptoms and advice.
- conversation: role-play a pharmacy or doctor visit — describe a problem, get advice.
- quiz focus: choosing *should* vs *have to / must*, and *mustn't* vs *don't have to*.

## Watch out
- You should to rest → You should rest
- I must not to work today (meaning it isn't necessary) → I don't have to work today
- She have to take medicine → She has to take medicine
