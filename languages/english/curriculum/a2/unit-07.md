# A2 · Unit 07 — Experiences

**Can-do:** I can talk about life experiences without saying exactly when, and ask others what they have done.

## Grammar focus
Present perfect with *ever / never / just / already / yet*, and a first look at present perfect vs past simple. Form it with *have / has + past participle*. Use it for experiences at some time in your life (time not stated) and for recent actions with a result now. *ever* asks "at any time?"; *never* = at no time; *just* = a very short time ago; *already* = sooner than expected; *yet* goes at the end of questions and negatives. Compare: use the **past simple** (not the present perfect) as soon as you give a finished time such as *last year* or *in 2020*.

- **Have** you **ever** been abroad?
- I'**ve never** tried sushi.
- She'**s just** finished her exam.
- We'**ve already** seen that film.
- They **haven't** arrived **yet**.
- He'**s won** several competitions.
- I'**ve been** to Rome. → I **went** to Rome **last spring**.

## Target vocabulary (10–12 items)
experience — something that happens to you and that you learn from
achievement — something good you succeed in doing with effort
adventure — an exciting or slightly dangerous activity
champion — the winner of a competition
record — the best result ever reached
explore — to travel through a place to learn about it
discover — to find or learn about something for the first time
amazing — extremely good or surprising
exciting — making you feel happy and full of energy
boring — not interesting at all
recently — a short time ago
so far — up to now

## Functions & phrases
- Asking about experience: "Have you ever…?", "What's the best place you've visited?"
- Sharing: "I've never done that.", "I've just started a new hobby."
- Reacting: "That sounds amazing!", "How exciting!"
- Checking progress: "Have you finished yet?", "I've already done it."

## Suggested activities
- reading: a short "things I've done" profile with several present-perfect sentences.
- conversation: an "experiences" survey — ask three partners "Have you ever…?"
- quiz focus: present perfect signals (*ever/never/just/already/yet*) vs past simple with a stated time.

## Watch out
- Did you ever been to Japan? → Have you ever been to Japan?
- I have seen him yesterday → I saw him yesterday
- She has already went home → She has already gone home
