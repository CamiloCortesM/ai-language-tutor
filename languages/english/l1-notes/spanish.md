# English ← Spanish speakers: what to watch

Contrastive notes for tutors teaching English to native Spanish speakers. Source: `docs/research/evidence-based-methods.md` §9.

## Pronunciation priority queue (train perception first)

1. **/ɪ/ vs /iː/** — *ship/sheep, live/leave, bit/beat*. Spanish has one /i/; this pair is priority #1.
2. **/æ/ vs /e/** — *man/men, bad/bed*.
3. **Initial s-clusters** — epenthetic vowel: "estart" for *start*, "eschool" for *school*.
4. **/θ/ and /ð/** — *think→"sink"*, *this→"dis"* (varies by dialect of Spanish).
5. **/b/–/v/** merged; final-consonant devoicing/dropping (*task*→"tas").
6. **Rhythm & schwa** — Spanish is syllable-timed; English reduces unstressed vowels to /ə/. Word stress errors wreck intelligibility more than any single consonant.

## Grammar traps

- **Dropped subject:** "Is raining" → *It's raining* (Spanish allows null subjects).
- **Third-person -s:** "she work" → *she works*.
- **Past simple vs present perfect:** "I have seen him yesterday" → *I saw him yesterday* (Spanish uses perfect more freely).
- **do-support:** "You like coffee?" / "I no understand" → *Do you like…? / I don't understand*.
- **Articles:** overuse ("the life is hard" → *life is hard*); a/an omission with professions ("she is teacher").
- **Adjective order/agreement:** "a car red", "interestings books".
- **Prepositions:** *depend ON* (not "of"), *married TO*, *arrive AT/IN*, *listen TO*.
- **Age/state with have:** "I have 25 years" → *I am 25*.

## False friends (top offenders)

| Spanish speaker says | thinking of | but it means | wanted word |
|---|---|---|---|
| actually | actualmente | in fact | currently |
| library | librería | biblioteca | bookstore |
| embarrassed | embarazada | avergonzado | pregnant |
| assist | asistir | ayudar | attend |
| carpet | carpeta | alfombra | folder |
| realize | realizar | darse cuenta | carry out |
| sensible | sensible | sensato | sensitive |
| exit | éxito | salida | success |

## Advantages to exploit

Thousands of Latin cognates make formal/academic register cheap (*information, decision, important*). Use cognate-rich texts to boost early comprehensibility — but drill the short Germanic core (phrasal verbs, *get/take/put*) hard, because there Spanish gives no help.
