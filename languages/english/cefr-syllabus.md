# Official English syllabus by CEFR level (A1–C1) — curriculum base

> Project research base (2026-07). Source for building `languages/english/curriculum/`.

Authority sources: **CEFR Companion Volume (Council of Europe, 2020)** for can-do statements; **English Grammar Profile (EGP)** and **English Vocabulary Profile (EVP)** from Cambridge/English Profile for criterial grammar and lexis per level; **Oxford 3000/5000** for vocabulary ranges; official Cambridge handbooks for exam formats. Cambridge baseline rule: **~200 guided hours per level jump**.

---

## Global can-do framework (CEFR, by skill)

The CEFR organizes each level into "Can do" descriptors across five curriculum-relevant skills: **Listening**, **Reading**, **Spoken Interaction**, **Spoken Production** and **Writing** (the 2020 Companion Volume adds **Mediation** and online interaction). Each successive level expands: topics (concrete → abstract), text length, input speed/accent, autonomy and accuracy.

---

## A1 — Beginner / Breakthrough

**Cumulative guided hours:** ~90–100 h.

**Can-do (by skill):**
- *Listening:* understands very basic words/phrases about self, family and immediate surroundings when speech is slow and clear.
- *Reading:* understands familiar names, words and very simple sentences (signs, catalogues).
- *Spoken interaction:* interacts simply if the interlocutor repeats/rephrases; asks and answers simple questions about immediate needs.
- *Spoken production:* uses simple phrases to describe where they live and people they know.
- *Writing:* writes a short, simple postcard; fills in forms with personal details.

**Grammar (EGP A1):** *to be* (affirmative/negative/interrogative), *have got*; present simple; present continuous; *there is/are*; articles *a/an/the*; plurals; subject pronouns and possessive adjectives; demonstratives *this/that/these/those*; *can* (ability/permission); imperatives; *some/any*; adverbs of frequency; *going to* (basic plans); *like/love/hate + -ing*; basic prepositions of place and time; *wh-* questions; connectors *and/but/because*; cardinal and ordinal numbers.

**Vocabulary:** ~**500–750** productive words. Oxford 3000: **1,061 words tagged A1**.

**Functions:** greeting and saying goodbye; introducing yourself and others; giving personal details; spelling; telling time, dates, prices; ordering basic food/drink; shopping; expressing likes/preferences; describing people and objects; talking about routines; asking for and giving simple directions.

**Topics:** personal information, family, home, daily routine, food and drink, shopping, weather, hobbies, colors, jobs, body, animals, places in town.

**Assessment (Cambridge Pre A1 Starters / toward A2 Key):** in practice A1 is assessed within A2 Key. Typical tasks: picture-word matching, one-word gap-fill, short dialogues.

## A2 — Elementary / Waystage

**Cumulative guided hours:** ~180–200 h (exam **A2 Key / KET**; Cambridge cites "~250 h").

**Can-do:**
- *Listening:* catches phrases and high-frequency vocabulary on personal topics (family, shopping, local work).
- *Reading:* reads short, simple texts; finds specific information (ads, menus, timetables).
- *Spoken interaction:* communicates in simple, routine tasks involving direct exchange of information.
- *Spoken production:* describes in a series of phrases their family, living conditions, education, job.
- *Writing:* writes short notes and messages; a simple personal letter (thanking, inviting).

**Grammar (EGP A2):** past simple (regular/irregular); past continuous; comparatives and superlatives; *be going to*; *will* (predictions/offers); *have to / must* (obligation); *should* (advice); zero/first conditional (basic); quantifiers *much/many/a lot of/(a) few/(a) little*; *too/enough*; infinitive of purpose; *would like*; *used to* (emerging); adverbs of manner; connectors *so/then/before/after*. **Present perfect** emerges in late A2 with *ever/never/just/already/yet*.

**Vocabulary:** ~**1,000–1,500** productive. Oxford 3000: **989 A2 words** (cumulative A1+A2 ≈ 2,050).

**Functions:** narrating past experiences and events; making plans and arranging to meet; comparing; giving simple opinions; suggesting and inviting; apologizing; making requests; describing symptoms; expressing quantity; talking about future plans.

**Topics:** work and study, travel and holidays, health, entertainment, transport, describing people, restaurants, basic technology.

**Assessment — A2 Key (KET):** 3 papers.
- *Reading & Writing* (60 min, 7 parts): matching, multiple choice, gap-fill, short writing (~25-word message).
- *Listening* (~30 min, 5 parts).
- *Speaking* (~8–10 min, paired, 2 parts): interview and collaborative task with visual prompt.

## B1 — Intermediate / Threshold

**Cumulative guided hours:** ~350–400 h (exam **B1 Preliminary / PET**).

**Can-do:**
- *Listening:* understands the main points of clear speech on familiar matters (work, leisure); catches the gist of radio/TV programs on topics of interest.
- *Reading:* understands texts in everyday or job-related language; descriptions of events, feelings and wishes in personal letters.
- *Spoken interaction:* deals with travel situations; enters unprepared into conversations on familiar topics.
- *Spoken production:* connects phrases to describe experiences, dreams, hopes; gives brief reasons and explanations for opinions and plans.
- *Writing:* writes simple connected texts on familiar topics; personal letters describing experiences and impressions.

**Grammar (EGP B1):** present perfect vs. past simple (contrast); present perfect continuous; past perfect (emerging); first and **second conditional**; *used to*; reported speech (statements); passive voice (present/past simple); modals of deduction/probability (*might/may/could*); defining relative clauses; question tags; verb + gerund/infinitive patterns; *so/such*; frequent phrasal verbs; future continuous (emerging).

**Vocabulary:** ~**2,000–2,750** productive. Oxford 3000: **906 B1 words** (cumulative ≈ 2,950).

**Functions:** expressing and justifying opinions; agreeing/disagreeing; giving advice; expressing preferences with reasons; describing experiences and feelings; narrating; complaining; expressing certainty/doubt; hypothesizing (2nd conditional); reporting what others said; talking about hopes and ambitions.

**Topics:** education, careers, media, environment, relationships, culture, sport, health and fitness, travel, technology, money.

**Assessment — B1 Preliminary (PET):** 4 papers.
- *Reading* (6 parts), *Writing* (2 parts: email + article/story), *Listening* (4 parts, ~30 min), *Speaking* (~12–17 min paired, 4 parts).

## B2 — Upper-Intermediate / Vantage

**Cumulative guided hours:** ~500–600 h (exam **B2 First / FCE**).

**Can-do:**
- *Listening:* understands extended talks and lectures; most news and TV programs in standard language.
- *Reading:* reads articles and reports on contemporary issues with stances; contemporary literary prose.
- *Spoken interaction:* interacts with fluency and spontaneity; takes an active part in discussions on familiar topics, defending viewpoints.
- *Spoken production:* presents clear, detailed descriptions on a wide range of subjects; explains advantages and disadvantages.
- *Writing:* writes clear, detailed texts (essays, reports) conveying information and arguing for/against.

**Grammar (EGP B2):** all conditionals incl. **third and mixed**; passive in all tenses and passive reporting structures; full reported speech; *wish / if only*; past perfect and past perfect continuous; perfect modals (*should/must/might/could have + p.p.*); non-defining relative clauses; causative *have/get sth done*; future perfect and future continuous; basic inversion; participle clauses; discourse markers; cleft sentences (emerging); wide range of phrasal verbs.

**Vocabulary:** ~**3,250–4,000** productive; comprehension ~5,000+. Oxford 3000 tops out at B2 (**1,569 B2 words**); Oxford 5000 begins.

**Functions:** developing arguments; expressing abstract ideas; speculating and hypothesizing; grading certainty; persuading; conceding points; expressing nuanced attitudes; summarizing; weighing pros and cons; expressing criticism/regret; negotiating.

**Topics:** current affairs, science and technology, environment and global issues, art and culture, business, psychology/relationships, media and advertising, crime and law, lifestyle.

**Assessment — B2 First (FCE):** 4 papers.
- *Reading & Use of English* (75 min, **7 parts, 52 questions**: multiple-choice cloze, open cloze, word formation, key word transformation, comprehension).
- *Writing* (80 min, 2 parts: compulsory essay + letter/report/review/article).
- *Listening* (~40 min, 4 parts, 30 questions).
- *Speaking* (14 min paired, 4 parts).

## C1 — Advanced / Effective Operational Proficiency

**Cumulative guided hours:** ~700–800 h (exam **C1 Advanced / CAE**).

**Can-do:**
- *Listening:* understands extended speech even when not clearly structured and when relationships are implicit; TV programs and films without effort.
- *Reading:* understands long, complex texts, literary and factual, appreciating style differences; specialized articles and long technical instructions.
- *Spoken interaction:* expresses ideas fluently and spontaneously without searching for words; uses language flexibly for social and professional purposes.
- *Spoken production:* presents clear, detailed descriptions of complex subjects, integrating sub-themes and closing with an appropriate conclusion.
- *Writing:* writes clear, well-structured texts on complex subjects, selecting the right register for the reader.

**Grammar (EGP C1):** command of mixed conditionals; emphatic inversion (*hardly…when, no sooner, not only…but also, seldom*); cleft sentences (*It was… / What I…*); advanced modality and hedging; ellipsis and substitution; nominalization; complex passive and reporting structures; subjunctive (*I suggest he be…*); advanced reduced/participle clauses; fronting; wide range of cohesive devices; nuanced use of aspect; high idiomatic and collocational range.

**Vocabulary:** ~**5,000–6,000+** productive; ~8,000 receptive. Oxford 5000: **1,391 C1 words** (the 2,000 words beyond the Oxford 3000 sit at B2–C1).

**Functions:** debating abstract/complex topics; structuring extended discourse; expressing subtle nuance; hedging and diplomacy; developing counterarguments; using irony/humor; adapting register; negotiating and mediating; giving nuanced feedback.

**Topics:** politics, economics, ethics, philosophy, academic subjects, global issues, science, literature, business, social issues, culture and identity.

**Assessment — C1 Advanced (CAE):** 4 papers.
- *Reading & Use of English* (90 min, **8 parts, 56 questions**).
- *Writing* (90 min, 2 parts: essay + letter/report/proposal/review).
- *Listening* (~40 min, 4 parts, 30 questions).
- *Speaking* (15 min paired, 4 parts).

**Curriculum usage notes:** (1) Grammar is *criterial* (first appears at that level), not exclusive — it is recycled and deepened at higher levels. (2) Productive vocabulary ranges are pedagogical estimates; Oxford numbers are exact per level. (3) For actionable per-lesson descriptor banks, EGP and EVP Online (englishprofile.org) are the granular source; the CEFR Companion Volume adds mediation and online-interaction scales.
